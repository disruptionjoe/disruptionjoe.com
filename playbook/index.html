00

Orientation

The Playbook turns AI adoption into designed room work: people practice on real tasks, compare what changed, name what got in the way, and leave with something useful enough to survive Monday.

people over prompts practice over theory

What the system makes possible

  • Real work in the room. Sessions produce readiness reads, use-case maps, workflow opportunities, priorities, and follow-through options.
  • Team-first adoption. The work is participatory enough to create buy-in and concrete enough to create outputs leaders can use.
  • Experience before explanation. Participants learn through direct contact with AI, not tool-demo theater.
You do not need a louder AI workshop. You need a better room for adoption.

01

Matrix Backbone

The matrix is the orientation layer. It keeps the room clear about what is being worked on and how the work needs to move.

ReadinessProblemsOpportunitiesApproachesCommitments

Examples from the backbone

  • Rows name the work. Readiness, Problems, Opportunities, Approaches, and Commitments keep adoption grounded in the room's real job.
  • Columns name movement. Explore, Judge, Align, and Decide describe how the room travels from raw material to owned next steps.
  • The matrix is not a cage. A good play can move across stages; one cell does not equal one exercise.

02

Session Arcs

An arc is the full journey of a session. It decides how a room opens, what it works through, and what kind of output should exist when people leave.

Arc examples

  • Friction to Opportunity. Turns workflow pain points into a structured map of AI-relevant opportunities.
  • Opportunity to Pilot Shape. Moves from possibility into a practical pilot outline with clearer risk and ownership.
  • Readiness to Activation. Helps a mixed-experience team find a shared starting point and first activation priorities.

03

Plays

A play is a bounded module of meaningful room work. Plays are outcome-facing: they produce something the room can use.

surfaceshapechoose

Play examples

  • Use-Case Harvest. Builds a broad inventory of places AI could help with actual team work.
  • Opportunity Clustering. Turns raw use cases into themes, readiness signals, and next intents.
  • Next-Step Menu. Creates concrete follow-through options before the room narrows to commitments.

04

Moves

Moves are the reusable interaction patterns inside plays. They create the energy, structure, and stage movement of the room.

Move examples

  • Prompt Crazy 8s. Participants use AI to generate distinct approaches fast, then curate the useful difference.
  • Heatmap the Outputs. The room evaluates AI-assisted outputs across dimensions like useful, surprising, risky, and realistic.
  • The Constraint Gauntlet. Real constraints enter after an idea forms, forcing the room to make it practical.

05

Assets

Assets are the support objects that go in front of participants or carry the session's output beyond the room.

Asset examples

  • Readiness worksheet. A structured surface for making the starting state visible.
  • Use-case backlog. A template for turning opportunity inventory into prioritized next work.
  • Evidence capture. A follow-up object for preserving what happened, what changed, and what should be tested next.

06

Learning Loop

The Playbook is alive. It gets stronger when delivery evidence shows which objects hold up in real rooms.

drafttesteddurablego-to

How the shelf improves

  • Draft. The object exists and can be used, but expectations stay clear.
  • Tested. A live run creates notes, evidence, and revision pressure.
  • Durable. The object works across situations and its outputs survive outside the room.

07

Assemble a Room

The method becomes real when the components lock together: arc, plays, moves, and assets arranged around the room's actual adoption problem.

ArcPlayMoveAsset

What happens in the room

  • The arc sets the journey. The session knows where the room starts and what kind of output it must land.
  • Plays do the work. Each module produces readiness, friction, opportunity, approach, or commitment output.
  • Moves create motion. Participants use AI directly, compare results, apply judgment, and translate activity into action.
