# Content Intake to Draft Bank

A workflow narrative for the Show the System in Motion section of the Claude CoWork proof page. This document drives the explainer video and supporting screenshots. Joe to refine before the video script locks.

## Idea capture

Ideas enter the system from many places: voice notes, inbox captures, a chat with the agent, an event Joe attended, a comment Joe wanted to make on someone else's work. The intake surface is loose by design. Joe should never have to format an idea to capture it.

The system distills raw captures into structured intake items. Each one carries the original context, who or what it was about, and a first-pass classification of where it should live (a content thread, a learning thread, a decision thread).

## Workflow routing

For content intake items, the system routes to the content agent. The content agent holds:

- A channel intelligence pack (what works on which channel, what voice the channel uses).
- A strategy pack (what Joe is actively driving and what the operating plan says).
- A brand and visual identity pack (so every draft carries Joe's identity).
- A bank state (so it knows what's already drafted and what's already been published).

The agent reads the intake item, picks the right channel, decides the right format (short post, longform article, panel, video script, etc.), and routes the item to the matching drafting workflow.

## Draft generation

The drafting workflow assembles a complete draft in Joe's voice. It:

- Builds the draft from the intake item and any relevant supporting context.
- Applies the channel-specific format (LinkedIn shape, blog shape, social shape).
- Pulls or generates the right visual asset to match the brand.
- Tags the draft with the channel, the publish window, and the originating intake item.
- Lands the finished draft in the content bank with status "ready, awaiting Joe publish."

The agent never publishes. That rule is permanent. Joe always reviews and publishes himself.

## Storage and reuse

The bank is the durable layer. Every draft sits there as a reusable asset:

- Joe can edit and publish any draft at any time.
- Drafts that don't get published stay in the bank as material for future ideas.
- The bank holds prior published content so the agent does not re-draft things Joe already shipped.
- A shipped-events log records what went where and when, feeding the strategy pack the next time it runs.

The same draft can be repurposed for a different channel with a one-line ask. The workflow does not have to be rebuilt.

## Outputs

- A draft in Joe's voice, on the right channel, with the right visual, ready for Joe to publish.
- A populated bank that protects Joe from blank-page mornings.
- A log of what went out and what landed, feeding the next round of strategy and intake.

## Why it matters

The point is not the draft. The point is building a system that consistently turns ideas into outputs without Joe rebuilding the workflow every time. Without the system, ideas die in voice memos and slack threads. With the system, ideas move through a repeatable process and become assets.

The pattern generalizes. The same intake-route-draft-bank shape applies to email outreach, longform writing, internal memos, talk proposals, and any other artifact that has a recurring shape.

## Before / After (mirrors the on-page summary)

- **Before.** Ideas are scattered and frequently lost.
- **After.** Ideas move through a repeatable process and become usable assets.
