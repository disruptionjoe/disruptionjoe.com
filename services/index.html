The Conversation Playbook

I've spent the past decade exploring, testing, and refining facilitation techniques to understand what actually makes conversations productive. This is the full playbook I use to design focused working sessions.

Each session is shaped around the people and situation in the room. The discovery call helps us choose the conversation that fits.

Figure It Out

Seven sessions to understand what's happening, uncover the real problem, and identify opportunities.

01 / Figure It Out

Find the Real Problem

A team, process, or initiative is stuck, but the important obstruction is unclear.

You leave withA shared friction map and the top problem or intervention point.

02 / Figure It Out

The Problem Framing Session

People see the issue differently or are solving different versions of it.

You leave withA clear problem definition and a better question to address.

03 / Figure It Out

Develop Shared Understanding

The group has information but no shared picture of the current situation.

You leave withA shared view of the evidence, disagreements, and important unknowns.

04 / Figure It Out

The Opportunity Map

The organization needs to identify and compare possible areas of opportunity.

You leave withAn opportunity map and the information a decision maker needs to choose.

05 / Figure It Out

AI: Where Do We Start?

The organization needs a sensible place to begin using AI.

You leave withA use-case map and a friction map that feed the next step: AI strategy.

06 / Figure It Out

Understand Our Customer

Decisions are being made from internal opinion rather than customer evidence.

You leave withA shared customer view that separates what is known from what the group is still guessing.

07 / Figure It Out

Team Health Check

The team feels off, but nobody has a shared view of why.

You leave withHealth ratings, the weak or divided areas, and one owned action for each selected area.

Get Organized

Fourteen sessions to turn ambition and good ideas into focus, structure, and shared direction.

08 / Get Organized

Prioritize the Backlog

Too many projects or requests are competing for limited capacity.

You leave withA ranked list of priorities with explicit tradeoffs.

09 / Get Organized

Team OKR Setting

A team needs objectives and measurable results connected to established business goals.

You leave withTwo or three objectives with draft measurable key results.

10 / Get Organized

Build the Roadmap

Goals are set and the work now needs sequencing.

You leave withAn objective-anchored roadmap with selected outcomes, dependencies, and three ambition tiers.

11 / Get Organized

Leadership Goal Setting

Leadership does not agree on what the business is trying to achieve.

You leave withThree to five prioritized goals, a clear focus strategy, and leading measures.

12 / Get Organized

Vision, Mission and Values

The organization lacks a shared statement of why it exists, where it is going, and how it behaves.

You leave withUsable vision and mission drafts, three to five values, and a clear refinement list.

13 / Get Organized

Kick Off the Project

A new initiative needs a shared starting foundation.

You leave withA project poster covering the goal, scope, owners, and risks.

14 / Get Organized

Clarify Strategic Focus

Direction keeps shifting or the organization is pursuing too many bets.

You leave withTwo or three committed bets, explicit tradeoffs, and an owned not-doing list.

15 / Get Organized

Define the Role

People disagree about what a role owns or how success should be judged.

You leave withA clear role purpose, ownership boundaries, decision rights, success measures, and key relationships.

16 / Get Organized

Plan for Uncertainty

A major unknown is preventing commitment or planning.

You leave withFour scenarios, no-regrets actions, early signals, and a decision made or tied to a clear trigger.

17 / Get Organized

Prepare for the Board

Leadership needs to align before an upcoming board meeting.

You leave withA one-page message map with the asks, key messages, hard questions, and named answerers.

18 / Get Organized

Run a Better Offsite

An offsite risks becoming presentations and unstructured discussion.

You leave withAn agreed offsite brief with goals, decisions, participants, constraints, and risks.

19 / Get Organized

AI: Define Our Strategy

AI experiments exist, but activity and priorities are scattered.

You leave withA defensible AI strategy with priorities, guardrails, outcome chains, measures, and owners.

20 / Get Organized

AI: Measuring Results

AI work is underway, but the organization cannot tell what is working or how to measure the value.

You leave withValue chains, evidence-backed decisions, and measures with baselines and expected timing.

21 / Get Organized

The RAID Review

A project lacks a shared view of its risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies.

You leave withA prioritized, owned RAID log with duplicates removed and categories clarified.

Make the Move

Five sessions to reach decisions, work through disagreement, and turn learning into action.

22 / Make the Move

Make a Tough Decision

A consequential choice is stuck among a bounded set of options.

You leave withA decision, or a ranked recommendation with tradeoffs, risks, and noted dissent.

23 / Make the Move

The Lightning Decision Jam

A known problem needs responses and an agreed course of action quickly.

You leave withA prioritized problem, promising solutions, and about three small, owned test steps.

24 / Make the Move

The Assumption Test

A promising direction needs challenge before major commitment.

You leave withThe critical assumptions, the evidence, and the smallest honest test for each.

25 / Make the Move

Build Alignment with Buy-In

Groups are operating from different pressures, needs, or interests.

You leave withA shared picture, clearer agreements, commitments, and unresolved issues.

26 / Make the Move

Blameless Retrospective

A project, launch, or incident produced results the group needs to learn from.

You leave withA shared timeline, useful lessons, and two or three owned changes with a check date.

Learn and Absorb

Three sessions to help people understand change, build useful capability, and put new ideas into practice.

27 / Learn and Absorb

Reset After Change

A reorganization, layoff, leadership change, or similar shift has left people uncertain.

You leave withA shared view of what continues, what ends, what changes now, and who owns the open questions.

28 / Learn and Absorb

Rally Behind the Story

A pivot, fundraise, strategy announcement, or similar change is coming, but people tell different versions of the story.

You leave withA confirmed core story, audience messages, hard-question answers, and an owner for each audience.

29 / Learn and Absorb

AI: Increasing Capability

The team has AI access, but people lack the skills or confidence to use it in real work.

You leave withPersonal AI use-case maps, ideas to borrow, and public commitments for the next month.
