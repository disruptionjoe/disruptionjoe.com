## Purpose

Use this file to check website drafts against the actual Disruption Joe brand voice and experience standard.

Do not treat the site as generic consulting website copy.

## Voice Standard

The voice should feel like a sharp operator with real room experience, strong taste, and a useful point of view.

Reference feel:

- Ann Handley clarity.
- Ogilvy direct-response discipline.
- Plain English.
- Concrete stakes.
- Specific promises.
- Strong rhythm.
- No filler.
- No hype.
- No "AI transformation" fog.

## Core Voice Expectations

- Practical, but not boring.
- Strategic, but not abstract.
- Warm, but not soft.
- Confident, but not inflated.
- Sales-aware, but not salesy.
- Opinionated enough to feel authored.
- Clear enough that a serious buyer immediately understands what Joe does, why it matters, and why the work is different.

## Avoid

- Generic AI consulting language.
- Over-polished SaaS voice.
- Motivational language without operational teeth.
- Empty verbs such as "unlock," "empower," "transform," and "future-proof" unless there is a very good reason.
- Em dashes.
- Repetitive sentence shapes unless the repetition is an intentional motif.
- Copy that sounds like it could belong to any AI enablement firm.

## Positioning To Preserve

Disruption Joe is not:

- a prompt-training shop
- an automation vendor
- an implementation firm
- a generic AI speaker

The work is about AI adoption through designed experiences:

- sessions
- methods
- activation systems
- interactive environments

These experiences help people actually change how they think, work, decide, and collaborate with AI.

## Buyer Feeling Sequence

The site should make the buyer feel:

1. This person understands the real adoption problem.
2. This is more serious and more useful than another AI workshop.
3. There is a method here.
4. The experience itself will change how our people behave.
5. I know what to do next.

## Design And Experience Standard

Assume the current brand direction remains grounded in:

- the black / tan visual world
- the existing brand fonts
- the section-by-section architecture

Do not replace the brand with a generic modern SaaS aesthetic.

The goal is to make the site:

- more interesting
- more memorable
- more experiential
- still grounded in the brand's underlying feel

The design should feel more like:

- a premium field manual
- a live facilitation environment
- a strategic operating notebook

than a normal agency website.

## Section Experience Standard

Each section should do more than say something. It should create an experience for the visitor.

Use, where appropriate:

- visual structure
- contrast
- interaction ideas
- diagrams
- field-note elements
- annotated frameworks
- cards
- fragments
- decision paths
- room-like moments

The page should feel designed by someone who knows how to hold attention in a live room.

The site should not just explain AI activation. It should perform AI activation.

## Per-Section Review Questions

For every section, check whether it has:

- a clear buyer job
- a felt moment or turn
- a specific claim
- a concrete proof point or example where possible
- a visual idea that makes the section more than text on a page
- a reason to keep scrolling
- a connection to the larger method

## Creative Standard

Be bolder than the safe version.

A creative swing that needs refinement is better than copy that comes back polished, flat, and forgettable.
