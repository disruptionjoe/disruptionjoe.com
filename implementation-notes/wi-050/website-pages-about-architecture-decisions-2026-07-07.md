## Site Spine

- Home
- Services
- Method
- AI Activation Playbook
- AI Enablement Architecture
- AI Accelerated Thinking
- About

## Page-Level Purpose

The About page answers one primary question:

> Who is the person behind this work, and would I trust him to help lead this?

It should not answer:

> What is Joe's biography?

The About page is the identity-revelation page.

Visitors should arrive here after practical trust has started forming through Home, Services, Method, Playbook, Enablement, or AI Accelerated Thinking.

## Page-Level Job

The About page reveals the person behind the operating model.

Homepage creates recognition.

Services creates commercial clarity.

Method creates trust in the operating model.

The Method subpages create proof and depth.

About should make the visitor think:

> Now I understand the judgment, standards, and personal operating system behind the work.

## Core Page Decision

About should not be a resume, founder story, chronology, or personal mythology page.

It should reveal Joe through:

- judgment
- standards
- principles
- working posture
- evidence
- links into deeper public surfaces

The page should help the visitor understand what it would feel like to work with Joe.

## Important Source Material

Use the constitutional thinking from the Thinking Wiki repo as reference material about Joe.

That material should help the builder understand:

- Joe's standards
- working principles
- beliefs about agency
- approach to truth and usefulness
- posture toward AI
- norms for collaboration
- respect for human judgment
- intellectual seriousness
- humility around uncertainty
- preference for practical public artifacts

But do not turn that material into manifesto copy.

Use it as private source material for tone, judgment, and structure.

The About page should feel like Joe's constitution is underneath the work, not pasted onto the page.

## Core About Message

Joe is not simply an AI trainer, facilitator, researcher, or consultant.

He is someone who builds environments where people can think, decide, coordinate, and execute better with AI.

The reason the work is different is not only skill.

It is a combination of:

- room craft
- systems thinking
- practical experimentation
- public artifact-building
- strong judgment
- respect for human agency
- willingness to keep learning in public

## Page-Level Posture

The page should feel:

- restrained
- warm
- direct
- grounded
- personal but not indulgent
- intelligent but not academic
- confident but not self-mythologizing
- human
- trustworthy
- quietly distinctive

It should not feel:

- like a resume
- like a founder origin story
- like a manifesto
- like a LinkedIn bio
- like personal branding theater
- like "look how smart Joe is"
- like a list of credentials
- like a movement pitch

Then:

> That person has unusual judgment.

Then:

> I would trust him in a room with my people, leaders, or organization.

## Section 1 - The Person Behind The Work

### Section Question

> Who is behind this?

### Section Purpose

Reveal Joe after the visitor has already seen the work.

This section should not begin with a childhood story, resume, or career chronology.

It should begin from the work the visitor has just experienced.

### Core Message

The rooms, methods, architectures, and artifacts on the site come from one person's way of working: practical, curious, rigorous, human-centered, and built around helping people use AI to become more capable.

### Architectural Decision

The About page should start by connecting the site back to Joe.

But it should not make Joe the hero of the whole website.

The visitor should feel:

> I have walked through the work. Now I am meeting the person who made it.

### Design / Experience Direction

Restrained reveal.

Could feel like:

- the studio signs its own name
- the lights come up on the person behind the rooms
- the workbench reveals the maker
- the operating model resolves into a human presence

But avoid making this too theatrical or self-important.

### What It Should Make Buyers Feel

Operators / Executives:

> There is a serious operator behind this, not just a website.

People / Learning / Community:

> This is a person I could imagine trusting with a room.

Innovation / Depth Buyers:

> The unusualness of the work has a real source.

### Supporting Insight

About is where identity reveals itself gradually after practical trust has formed.

It should not front-load personality.

It should explain the person once the visitor already has reason to care.

### Avoid

- biography opening
- resume opening
- "I have always believed..." opening
- self-mythology
- making the entire site suddenly about Joe
- sounding like a founder story
- making the reveal too precious

## Section 2 - The Five Forms Of Trust

### Section Question

> What kind of trust should I have in Joe?

### Section Purpose

Build the specific kinds of trust a buyer needs before inviting Joe into an organization.

### Core Message

Working with Joe requires more than believing he knows AI.

It requires believing he has the competence, judgment, character, humility, and stability to help people and organizations move through change.

### Trust Forms

The core trust model should include five forms:

1. Competence
2. Judgment
3. Character
4. Humility
5. Stability

Do not casually add a sixth form unless there is a clear reason.

If "Direction" appears, it should support the five-form model rather than confuse it.

### Competence

Visitors should believe:

> Joe can actually do the work.

Signals may include:

- facilitation experience
- AI activation work
- public artifacts
- systems built
- evidence from the Method pages
- ability to translate ideas into usable experiences

### Judgment

Visitors should believe:

> Joe knows what to do, what not to do, and when to slow down.

Signals may include:

- careful claims
- respect for risk
- no hype posture
- ability to distinguish novelty from usefulness
- protecting professional standards
- ability to work with leaders and mixed-readiness groups

### Character

Visitors should believe:

> Joe is trying to help people become more capable, not sell fear or perform expertise.

Signals may include:

- generosity
- respect for human agency
- practical usefulness
- care for the room
- public work
- honest uncertainty

### Humility

Visitors should believe:

> Joe can lead without pretending to know everything.

Signals may include:

- learning in public
- testing what breaks
- adapting methods
- respecting client context
- avoiding overclaiming
- asking better questions

### Stability

Visitors should believe:

> Joe has enough steadiness to hold complexity.

Signals may include:

- clear standards
- repeatable methods
- calm tone
- structured thinking
- disciplined experimentation
- ability to work across uncertainty without becoming chaotic

### Architectural Decision

This section should not simply list traits.

It should show how each form of trust matters to client work.

### Design / Experience Direction

Could use a compact grid or shelf of trust forms.

Each trust form should be short and specific.

Every word should earn its place.

### What It Should Make Buyers Feel

Operators / Executives:

> This person has judgment and stability, not just ideas.

People / Learning / Community:

> This person has humility and character, not just capability.

Innovation / Depth Buyers:

> This person has competence and originality without overclaiming.

### Supporting Insight

The page should build trust in Joe's judgment more than trust in Joe's resume.

### Avoid

- generic trait cards
- "trusted advisor" cliches
- long credentials
- overclaiming
- making the five forms feel like corporate values
- adding too many traits
- saying "five" and showing six without explanation

## Section 3 - The Working Constitution

### Section Question

> What principles guide the way Joe works?

### Section Purpose

Use constitutional thinking from the Thinking Wiki repo to reveal the standards beneath the work.

This section should explain the operating principles without becoming a manifesto.

### Core Message

The work is guided by a practical constitution: help people preserve agency, improve judgment, build useful capability, and create systems that make better thinking and coordination possible.

### Source Guidance

Use the Thinking Wiki constitutional material as reference.

Look for principles related to:

- human agency
- usefulness
- truthfulness
- epistemic humility
- public artifacts
- positive-sum work
- respect for complexity
- better thinking
- better coordination
- better execution
- learning in public
- avoiding hype
- building systems that help people act

Do not quote or expose raw internal material unless Joe explicitly approves.

Translate the principles into public-facing trust.

### Architectural Decision

This section should show that Joe's work is governed by standards.

It should not become:

- a doctrine
- a manifesto
- a moral lecture
- a Church of AI bridge
- a research philosophy dump

### Possible Public Principles

The public-facing version may communicate principles like:

- AI adoption starts with people, not prompts.
- Capability matters more than tool access.
- Judgment should be strengthened, not bypassed.
- The room should make people more capable, not smaller.
- Useful systems beat impressive ideas.
- The method should keep learning as the tools change.
- Public artifacts are proof of work, not performance.
- The goal is better thinking, coordination, and execution.

### Design / Experience Direction

This section could feel like a small set of working standards on the wall of the studio.

Not a big manifesto.

Not a scroll of commandments.

Possible visual:

- standards shelf
- field notes
- small constitutional cards
- workbench principles
- margin notes
- visible operating rules

### What It Should Make Buyers Feel

Operators / Executives:

> There are standards behind the work.

People / Learning / Community:

> This person will respect the humans in the room.

Innovation / Depth Buyers:

> This person has a serious theory of agency and capability without turning it into abstraction.

### Supporting Insight

The About page should reveal the person through the standards he works by.

The constitutional material should shape the page's judgment, not become the page's content.

### Avoid

- manifesto energy
- moral grandstanding
- dumping internal constitutional text
- making Church of AI part of the commercial path
- sounding academic
- making the section too long
- presenting principles that do not connect to client work

## Section 4 - Proof Of The Person

### Section Question

> What evidence supports this picture of Joe?

### Section Purpose

Anchor the identity reveal in evidence.

This section should prevent About from becoming all claim and no proof.

### Core Message

The same patterns that show up in the work also show up in Joe's public artifacts, client work, and way of building.

### Evidence Types

Possible evidence anchors:

- AI Activation work
- Playbook / room design
- Enablement Architecture
- AI Accelerated Thinking
- GitHub
- public artifacts
- LinkedIn professional writing
- research / systems work
- facilitation experience
- prior operator or leadership experience
- client-safe examples
- talks, posts, or public work

### Architectural Decision

About should include evidence, but not become a credentials page.

Evidence should be compact and selected.

The visitor should be able to go deeper if they want.

### Design / Experience Direction

Could use a small "evidence shelf" or "public doors" section.

It should route visitors to:

- LinkedIn
- GitHub
- AI Accelerated Thinking
- Method
- selected public artifacts

The links should feel like controlled doorways, not a dump.

### What It Should Make Buyers Feel

Operators / Executives:

> There is enough substance here to trust the person.

People / Learning / Community:

> The public work supports the human posture.

Innovation / Depth Buyers:

> There are artifacts to inspect if I want to go deeper.

### Supporting Insight

About should route curious visitors to deeper external surfaces in a way that does not overwhelm or trigger resistance.

The page should let people who are already bought in understand why Joe ticks.

### Avoid

- long resume
- credential wall
- link dump
- overexposing research
- making GitHub the main proof for every buyer
- making About compete with AI Accelerated Thinking
- leaving trust claims unsupported

## Section 5 - What It Is Like To Work With Joe

### Section Question

> What would it be like to work with Joe?

### Section Purpose

Translate identity into working relationship.

This section should help buyers imagine Joe in the room, in planning, and in difficult moments.

### Core Message

Working with Joe should feel clear, practical, generous, thoughtful, and useful.

He brings structure without making the room rigid.

He brings depth without making people feel small.

He brings originality without losing the business problem.

### What This Section Should Communicate

Visitors should understand that Joe is likely to be:

- a strong facilitator
- a practical thinking partner
- good with ambiguity
- respectful of people
- serious about outcomes
- willing to challenge assumptions
- careful with claims
- useful in a room
- capable of translating complexity into action

### Architectural Decision

This section should be about the working experience, not personality traits in the abstract.

It should help buyers answer:

> Would I trust this person with my leaders, my team, or my organization?

### Design / Experience Direction

Could be compact, maybe almost like a working agreement.

Possible forms:

- short field notes
- working principles
- "in the room" statements
- small examples of how Joe behaves
- what Joe brings / what Joe avoids

### What It Should Make Buyers Feel

Operators / Executives:

> He can be useful without creating noise.

People / Learning / Community:

> He can hold a room safely and productively.

Innovation / Depth Buyers:

> He can handle unusual work without becoming unserious.

### Supporting Insight

The buyer does not only need to know Joe's background.

They need to know whether they would want Joe in the room.

### Avoid

- personality branding
- vague warmth
- overpromising
- making Joe sound like a guru
- making the working relationship too soft
- saying "trusted partner" without evidence
- making this a sales CTA disguised as About

## Section 6 - Continue Into The Work

### Section Question

> Where should I go from here?

### Section Purpose

Route visitors based on what they now want to understand.

This section can offer next paths without turning About into a hard conversion page.

### Core Message

If visitors want to understand the work, explore Method.

If they want to understand the deeper studio, explore AI Accelerated Thinking.

If they want to talk about their organization, contact Joe.

### Possible Paths

#### Method

For visitors who want to understand the operating model.

#### AI Accelerated Thinking

For visitors who want to understand the deeper studio and public artifacts.

#### LinkedIn

For visitors who want current professional thinking.

#### GitHub

For visitors who want public technical or research artifacts.

#### Contact Joe

For visitors ready to start a planning conversation.

### Architectural Decision

About can include Contact, but it should not become a sales page.

The page should end with calm orientation.

### Design / Experience Direction

Simple, controlled paths.

No big pressure CTA.

Maybe:

- Explore the Method
- Visit the Studio
- Read current thinking on LinkedIn
- View public artifacts
- Contact Joe

### What It Should Make Buyers Feel

Operators / Executives:

> I know where to go if I want to talk.

People / Learning / Community:

> I can keep understanding the method before reaching out.

Innovation / Depth Buyers:

> I can inspect the deeper work.

### Supporting Insight

About is a routing page for people who have already developed curiosity or trust.

It should not force one next action.

### Avoid

- hard-selling
- turning About into Contact
- link dumping
- over-routing to research
- making external surfaces compete with the commercial path
- making Church of AI a primary route

## About-Level Supporting Insights

### About Is The Identity-Revelation Page

The page should reveal identity after practical trust has formed.

It should not lead the site.

It should deepen the visitor's understanding of why the work is different.

### About Is Not Biography

The page should not explain Joe by chronological life story.

It should explain Joe by judgment, standards, working principles, evidence, and what he is like to work with.

### About Should Use Constitutional Thinking Carefully

The Thinking Wiki constitutional material should be used as reference for the person behind the work.

It should guide:

- standards
- voice
- working principles
- trust
- humility
- agency
- public artifact posture

It should not be copied into the page as internal doctrine.

### About Should Build Trust, Not Awe

The visitor should not think:

> Wow, Joe is impressive.

They should think:

> I would trust Joe with this work.

### About Should Humanize Without Flattening

The page should be personal enough to make Joe real.

It should not flatten him into a generic consultant bio.

It should also not overexpose the deepest weirdness before the visitor is ready.

### About Should Let Curious Visitors Go Deeper

About is a good place to route to:

- LinkedIn
- GitHub
- AI Accelerated Thinking
- public artifacts
- Method

But those should be controlled pathways, not a public sprawl.

### About Should Preserve Commercial Trust

The page should help commercial buyers trust Joe more.

It should not make them wonder whether they have wandered into a research project, movement, or personal ideology.

### Best Buyer Reaction

The visitor should think:

> Now the site makes sense. The work, the methods, and the deeper artifacts all come from the same kind of judgment.

Then:

> I would trust this person in a serious room.

- biography
